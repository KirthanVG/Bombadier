Where the numbers come from

  • Blast effects use the scaling formulas in The Effects of Nuclear Weapons (Glasstone & Dolan, 1977), published by the US government.
  • Population comes from WorldPop's 2020 estimates on a 100 m grid, open data from the University of Southampton.
  • Weapon yields are from public records. Some are official claims or estimates and are marked with ~.
  • Maps are Esri imagery and street tiles, shown with the open-source Leaflet library.

Casualty rates per damage zone are simplified, so all figures are illustrative estimates, not predictions.

Welcome to Bombadier!

An interactive map that shows what a bomb would do if it went off anywhere on Earth, from a hand grenade to the Tsar Bomba, the largest explosion ever set off.

How it works

  1. Pick one of 200+ weapons from the panel on the left.
  2. Click anywhere on the map to drop it.
  3. Watch the damage rings spread and read the casualty estimate in the top right.

Please keep in mind

Every figure here is a rough estimate, not a prediction. The model is deliberately simple: it leaves out terrain, weather, buildings, time of day, warning time, fallout and emergency response. Population shows where people live, not where they are at any given moment. Bombadier! is for education and curiosity only. Data sources are listed at the bottom of the weapons panel.

Have fun bombing!
